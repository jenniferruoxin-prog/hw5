1. How I evaluate the agents' performance

Ticket 101 (Bulldog tee): strong investigation and safety; it took a human to define "done".
The Boss sent the ticket to Inventory first, as planned. Inventory found CC-TEE-WHITE size S at 0 on hand and Bulldog Print Co blocked by invoice 501 ($840, 3 days overdue on the 2026-08-31 shop date). Accounting checked the invoice and cash, then filed approval request #1 instead of paying. After I approved it (Cash tab, row 1: $3,400 → $2,560), a rerun found the vendor could ship again. Accounting then filed request #3, an $8 purchase order (1 × $8 unit cost); I approved it (row 3: $160 → $152). Customer Service kept the customer update as a draft through four versions and never sent it.
The weak spot was the ending. With the tee arriving 2026-09-05 and desk.date_today fixed at 2026-08-31, Inventory refused to call the order fulfilled, and the Boss kept 101 in_progress for two runs. That was the right call, because nothing proved the tee had arrived. But "resolved" was never defined. It only closed after I recorded a human decision: done means the order is placed and paid, the 9/5 arrival is recorded, and the draft is saved. Five runs and ≈203k tokens is a lot for a one-tee order.

Ticket 102 (Rent due): the cleanest money workflow, held back by a tool gap I caused.
Facilities was called first and used the shop date: $2,400 to Elm City Properties, due 2026-09-02, 2 days out. Accounting found no earlier rent payment and filed request #2, projecting $160 left once both pending requests were paid. The Boss parked the ticket at awaiting_approval. After my approval (Cash tab, row 2: $2,560 → $160), the second run tripped. approve_payment had moved next_due to 2026-10-02 as designed, but get_rent_due didn't say the payment covered September 2. So Facilities correctly flagged "notice says Sep 2, lease says Oct 2". The Boss set the ticket to in_progress, and Customer Service drafted a clarification to the landlord. Once the tool listed each rent payment with the due date it covered, run 3 resolved it. Accounting declined to request rent a second time, and the circular-delegation guard refused a Facilities → Boss hand-back. The agents behaved well; the extra run came from a missing fact in my tool.

Ticket 103 (Hoodie discount): very careful with facts, and it correctly refused to make a pricing decision it had no authority for.
Inventory found 8 of 20 CC-HOOD-NAVY size M (shortfall 12). Accounting gave the economics: $22 cost, $58 list, $36 (62.1%) margin at list, and $22 as the hard floor. No discount policy exists in the database, so the Boss set the ticket to blocked and later awaiting_approval rather than inventing a percentage. The ticket was also tied to the others through cash. In run 1 the vendor was still blocked by invoice 501 from ticket 101. By the later runs checking was $152 (Cash tab), so restocking all 12 (12 × $22 = $264) was impossible, and at most 6 more ($132) would have fit. After my decision (8 in-stock hoodies at 10% off, $52.20, no restock), Accounting worked out the margin: $52.20 − $22 = $30.20 per hoodie (57.9%). Customer Service drafted the offer, and a second decision line telling the Boss when the ticket counts as done let it resolve. Weak spot: run 1 cost ≈79k tokens. Accounting asked the Boss about policy and the Boss asked Accounting back (chain Boss › Accounting › Boss › Accounting, depth 3), and both Inventory and Accounting called check_stock_and_price for the same row.

3. What would have been simpler as one agent with tools

Ticket 102. It is one straight line: get_rent_due → compare with desk.date_today → list_payments (duplicate check) → get_cash_balance → request_payment_approval → wait → verify. In run 1 Facilities made exactly one tool call, and its whole job was reading one lease row. One "operations" agent with those five tools would make the same calls without the Boss → Facilities and Boss → Accounting round trips. On this ticket the three agents never disagreed, and the multi-agent setup cost ≈101k tokens across three runs. A single agent is just as safe, because the safety lives in the tools, not the org chart: approve_payment is still hidden from it, the server still rechecks cash, and approval still belongs to me.
Partly 103's fact-finding as well. Inventory and Accounting each called check_stock_and_price for CC-HOOD-NAVY/M in the same run, and the policy question bounced Boss → Accounting → Boss → Accounting. One agent would have read the row once and stopped at "no policy, human needed" sooner.
Where multiple agents earned their cost: 101, because it crossed stock, vendor, invoice, cash, purchasing and a customer message, and separate roles kept "can the vendor ship?" apart from "should we pay?". Also 103's final step, where Accounting checked my $52.20 price on its own ($30.20 margin) before Customer Service wrote it into the offer.

4. Three new problems this team could solve with the tools we built

  1. A Crest Mug order. The database already has CC-MUG-CREST (OS) at 0 on hand. A customer order runs like 101: Inventory → check_stock_and_price (shortfall), check_vendor_shipping → Elm City Gifts ("mugs and small goods") has no open invoice, so it can ship, with a 3-day lead (arrival 2026-09-03). Accounting → request_payment_approval(kind="purchase_order") at $3.50 per mug, which fits in the $152 left (up to 43 mugs). Then a human approves, Customer Service saves a draft with the arrival date, and a human decision says what "done" means, as on 101.
  2. The October rent. After 102, lease 1 shows next_due = 2026-10-02 and $2,400. Facilities can read that, and Accounting can prove the shop can't pay it: with $152 in checking, request_payment_approval refuses because it would leave −$2,248. So the team can raise the problem early, say exactly how much is missing, and block an overdraft. That is the "no negative balance" rule working on a real obligation. (Fixing the shortfall is problem 5C below.)
  3. A bulk discount on stock we already have. For example, a club wants 15 Yale Caps (CC-HAT-BLUE, 40 on hand, $6 cost, $22 list). Inventory confirms there's no shortfall. Accounting reports the margin at any proposed price and flags anything at or below $6. I record the approved price with record_human_decision, and Customer Service drafts the offer. This is ticket 103 without the stock and cash problems, so it could close in one run.

5. Three new problems this team could not solve, and what it would need

  1. Receiving the shipment (ticket 101's real ending). The tee is "expected 2026-09-05", but nothing can move desk.date_today, mark a purchase order shipped or received, or add the unit to inventory.qty. Today "ordered" is worked out from the payment record, and "done" needed my decision.
    Needed: a purchase_orders / shipments table (ordered, shipped, received dates and quantity); tools get_shipment_status, record_inventory_receipt (human or scanner confirmed) and update_purchase_order_status; a Receiving / Logistics agent that matches what arrived against the purchase order and reopens 101 if the shipment is short or late; and a human-controlled tool to advance the shop date for simulations.
  2. Deciding the discount without me (ticket 103). The team can show the $22 floor and the $36 list margin, but "don't sell below cost" isn't a pricing policy. That's why 103 sat at blocked / awaiting_approval until I picked 10%.
    Needed: a discount_policy table (minimum margin %, volume tiers such as 10 or more units, eligible customer types such as student orgs, approver limits); tools get_discount_policy, calculate_allowed_discount(sku, qty, customer_type) and request_discount_exception; and a Pricing / Sales agent that applies the policy and only escalates exceptions. With that, Yale AI Club's 20 hoodies would come back as a policy-approved offer, not a question to a manager.
  3. Bringing money in: sales, deposits, refunds (the Cash tab only goes down). The Cash tab is three withdrawals and no deposits, and that is the root of 103's "$264 restock won't fit" and the October rent shortfall above. If Yale AI Club pays $52.20 × 8 = $417.60, nothing records it, so the team can't see that the club's own deposit could fund the restock. It also can't take a deposit, track an unpaid customer balance, or refund Tauhid if the tee is late.
    Needed: customers, sales_orders, receipts and refunds tables; tools create_sales_order, record_customer_payment (which raises cash_accounts.balance), issue_refund (human-approved, like payments) and get_customer_balance; a Sales / Order Management agent; and Accounting extended to revenue, receivables and cash forecasting (for example "after the Yale AI Club deposit, can we afford the 12-hoodie purchase order and the Oct 2 rent?").

Overall

The team did best where the database held a clear obligation and a clear rule, as on 102 (once its tool told the whole story). It was most valuable at not doing unsafe things:

  • every payment went through me (the Cash tab shows three rows, all approved by Jennifer Zhang)
  • the balance never went below $152
  • no customer, landlord or vendor was contacted (8 drafts saved, 0 sent)
  • no discount or delivery was made up
Its weak points were the edges of the model: a real-world event the database can't record (101's delivery), a policy that doesn't exist (103's discount), and cash that can only go out (all three). It is also expensive: 12 runs and ≈527k tokens for three small tickets. The biggest lesson for me is that each pause was useful. The agents turned vague tickets into checked facts plus one clear question for a human, and the fixes we made during the run (the rent-payment history, the order status, the human-decision tool) came straight from those pauses.
